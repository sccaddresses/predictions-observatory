Methodology & governance

Designed to be wrong honestly.

A prediction project earns trust by preserving the information state that existed before an event, measuring the forecast after the event, and resisting the temptation to explain away misses once the answer is known.

Core contract

Freeze first. Score later.

Pre-race artefacts retain source timestamps, hashes and model identities. Post-race processing can add results and evaluation, but it does not rewrite the forecast that existed before the event.

Separation principle

Sporting evidence and market evidence are different layers.

The market-blind model is intended to estimate sporting probability independently. Exchange and bookmaker prices belong to a later comparator layer. That means agreement and disagreement can both be measured rather than market consensus silently leaking into the model.

Market remains read-only
Point-in-time pipeline

The chain of custody for a forecast.

The methodology is intentionally event-sourced. Every important state can be reconstructed from stored artefacts rather than memory or retrospective narrative.

01

Acquire

Racecards and conditions are captured from configured providers with retrieval timestamps. Raw source payloads are stored before feature generation.

02

Structure

The seven ITV7 legs are identified deterministically. Runner identity, form, official rating, weight, draw, trainer, jockey and provenance remain linked.

03

Model independently

SCC Core, the transparent baseline and Quant Shadow remain distinct evidence streams. Frozen is a publication state, not a model family. A future historical ensemble will also be versioned independently.

04

Freeze

Pre-event predictions become immutable artefacts. A later change requires a new version and a new forward test, not a corrected past.

05

Observe the market

When available, read-only market snapshots are captured separately. Model-market probability gaps are diagnostics, not proof of value.

06

Settle and score

Provider results are matched back to race and runner identifiers. Winners, placings, non-finishes, log loss and Brier score are recorded without silently dropping awkward outcomes.

07

Accumulate evidence

The public ledger grows prospectively. Only after the predefined evidence threshold does a performance-driven retuning review become appropriate.

Evaluation metrics

A winner is useful. A probability is richer.

Hit rates answer whether the top selection won. Probability scores ask whether the entire field distribution was sensible — including how confidently the model was wrong. SCC Core historical selections remain ranking-only unless a full probability field was frozen prospectively.

1

Winner hit

The easiest measure to understand: did the model's selected runner win? Useful, but it discards almost all of the probability information.

3

Top-three finish

A secondary ranking measure that captures near-misses without pretending a placing is the same thing as a win.

ln

Multiclass log loss

Rewards probability placed on the actual winner and punishes excessive confidence in horses that lose. Lower is better.

B²

Multiclass Brier score

Measures squared error across the full field probability vector. Lower is better and calibration matters.

Governance threshold

One card can expose a bug. It cannot justify performance chasing.

The default gate is 50 fully reconciled ITV7 cards / 350 race legs before a planned performance-driven retuning review. Non-retuning diagnostic checkpoints occur at 5 cards (data quality), 10 (calibration), and 25 (segments / failure modes). Engineering fixes, data-quality fixes and leakage corrections are allowed earlier because they protect validity rather than chase the last result.

Feature roadmap

From simple signals toward racing context.

The current models are intentionally labelled experimental or provisional. The roadmap favours stronger data and walk-forward validation over false precision.
